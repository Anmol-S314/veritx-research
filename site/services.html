The offering

Three services, one methodology.

One methodology underneath all three: a 30-point audit discipline, silicon-anchored measurement, known-answer gates, and a reproducible pipeline. Services are how we monetise it; the methodology is the moat.

Service A — Credibility audit (entry product)

We review a customer's NoC/interconnect simulation pipeline against the discipline in our audit playbook — a 30-point checklist derived from 19 documented ways interconnect models produce confident, wrong numbers.

  • Deliverable: a written report classifying every claim in their pipeline as calibrated, uncalibrated, or known-false, with the size of the error where we can measure it — plus the checklist, so their next pipeline has it wired in.
  • Who it's for: AI-chip startups with a NoC claim in their deck; semiconductor VCs doing diligence before a cheque.
  • Price: ₹8–25L (~$10–30K) per audit, 2–4 weeks. Target: 10–20 customers/year.

Service B — Calibration and re-measurement (the serious one)

We build a silicon-anchored measurement the way we did for ourselves: cycle-accurate DRAM simulation, router energy calibrated against published silicon, measured floorplans instead of guessed rulers, and known-answer gates with exact answers.

  • Deliverable: a re-measured headline number the customer can put in front of a customer or investor, with the full evidence trail behind it. The "5.4× → honestly 244 tok/s, and here's the DRAM efficiency it assumes" move, done for their chip.
  • Who it's for: hyperscaler/ODM procurement validating a supplier's performance claims before commit; AI-silicon teams pre-funding.
  • Price: ₹40L–1.2Cr (~$50–150K) per engagement, 6–12 weeks. Target: 5–10/year.

Service C — On-silicon verification

We write kernel-level microbenchmarks that check an interconnect claim against actual hardware — for example, whether multicast wall-time is flat in fanout, the assumption a KV-serving result rests on. We rent the card, run the benchmark, and the answer decides the claim.

  • Deliverable: a measurement campaign report with the hardware data, verdicts, and the exact kernel used — reproducible by anyone.
  • Who it's for: any team whose architecture rests on an assumption about real silicon behavior. Measuring assumptions on real hardware instead of asserting them is the single most defensible thing we sell.
  • Price: ₹8–20L (~$10–25K) per measurement campaign. Target: as many as the customer base sustains.

What we explicitly do NOT sell

  • "AI-native NoC IP" — our own research shows topology is not the lever for transformer accelerators, and the one mechanism we found is already shipped by a major vendor. Dead phrase; it appears in no proposal.
  • Patents as the asset — nothing in our methodology is patentable as-is. The moat is a methodology + a calibrated toolchain + a pipeline of cheap, trained labour.
  • Certification claims — we sell formal-verification services (property writing, proof strategy, coverage studies), never ASIL-B-style certification stamps. That is a certification-body process, not ours.

First move (90 days): five free or at-cost audits for startups we can name as reference customers, producing public (redacted) case studies. The playbook is the product; the first five audits are the proof it works on someone else's pipeline, not just ours.
