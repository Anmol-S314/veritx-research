The moat

The methodology.

We did not learn this from a textbook. We catalogued 19 distinct ways our own interconnect models produced plausible, confident, wrong numbers — and the discipline that caught each one. That catalogue is the Audit Playbook: a 30-point checklist where every point is a claim → what must be true → the check that kills it → our past failure as the worked example.

The honesty clause: the checklist includes our own past failures by name. We are one rushed audit away from being the cautionary tale — so every client engagement runs through the same gates we imposed on ourselves. That is the product.

The five disciplines

1. Known-answer gates

An exact, derivable answer pinned before any measurement — g−1 = 7, a crossover at exactly 8/3. If the pipeline can't reproduce the known answer, nothing it says about the unknown is credible.

2. Silicon anchors

Every energy and latency number is tied to published silicon measurements — router energy calibrated 1.37× against FlooNoC, cycle-accurate DRAM simulation, measured floorplans. Guessed rulers are a pitfall, not a parameter.

3. Pre-committed gates (0–4)

Pass/fail criteria written before the experiment runs: prior-art pass, known-answer reproduction, external anchor, and only then a ranking verdict. Gates decide; experiments serve them.

4. Reproducible pipeline

Docker + CI, seeded runs that are bit-identical, results as pipeline artifacts. A number a client can regenerate is a number they can trust; a number only we can produce is a claim.

5. Classified, then corrected

Every claim in scope gets one of three verdicts: calibrated, uncalibrated, known-false — with the magnitude of the error where measurable. "Your pipeline is wrong, and here is how wrong" is the deliverable.

Why tool vendors can't sell this

Arteris and Synopsys sell simulators. Their incentive is that the simulator is used, not that its numbers are right. Verification consultancies check RTL correctness, not model credibility. The audit category does not exist yet — we are creating it, with the first five (free or at-cost) audits as the proof it works on someone else's pipeline, not just ours.
