The problem

Nobody sells the "measure the claims" service.

Tool vendors (Arteris, Synopsys, Cadence) sell simulators — they have no incentive to tell you the number is wrong. Verification consultancies check RTL correctness, not model credibility. So a startup's Series-A data can rest on a hardcoded constant, a flit size in bytes instead of bits, or a sensitivity sweep that is flat by construction — and no one catches it until the customer does.

We can say this with evidence: our own study catalogued 18 distinct ways our NoC models lied to us — and our topology verdict flipped four times while we held them. The list is not exotic:
